## Project at a Glance

- one hypothetical metro station;
- ventilation modeling;
- passenger heat generation;
- train heat generation;
- airflow and air renewal;
- Differential Equations;
- Calculus II;
- mathematical modeling; and
- logical reasoning.

This repository documents an award-winning undergraduate project that studied
the ventilation and thermal conditions of a hypothetical metro station. The
model connected minimum passenger occupancy, heat generated by passengers and
an arriving train, airflow, air renewal, station volume, and temperature
through multiple coupled differential equations.

The competition emphasized assumption definition, logical reasoning,
mathematical modeling, and problem decomposition. The objective was to
translate a complex physical situation into a coherent mathematical system,
not to produce a construction-ready engineering design.

## Project Versions

### Version 1: Faithful reconstruction

[`original-model/`](original-model/) is a faithful reconstruction of the
original mathematical reasoning. It preserves:

- the problem statement;
- mathematical reasoning;
- assumptions;
- variables;
- modeling methodology; and
- logical decomposition.

Numerical values, example parameters, station dimensions, the Python
implementation, and the expanded documentation were recreated for this
repository.

### Version 2: Future redevelopment

[`remodeled-model/`](remodeled-model/) is reserved for a future computational
redevelopment using modern numerical simulation and software engineering
practices. It will build on the preserved reasoning while remaining clearly
separate from the historical account of the 2024 project.

## Why I Revisited This Project

Over the past six months, I have been studying Linear Algebra continuously as
part of my undergraduate research. Professor Gilbert Strang's work has been
one of my main references, together with the guidance of my advisor, Professor
Marco Antônio Menezes. During the academic break, I decided to return to this
project with a broader mathematical perspective.

My objective is to deepen the foundations of the model while beginning my
studies in scientific computing and data science libraries. I am using
academic references alongside AI as a learning tool, always treating the
technology as support for study, verification, and experimentation rather
than as a substitute for mathematical understanding.

I want to build a strong foundation in mathematics, scientific computing, and
data science before eventually specializing in AI Engineering. This repository
is not a copy of the original award-winning submission. It is a continuation
of the idea that submission began: the original project inspired this new
implementation and the decision to study the problem again with better tools
and deeper knowledge.

The competition taught me that the most important part of mathematical
modeling is not simply obtaining a final numerical answer. It is learning how
to interpret a real-world problem, define useful assumptions, connect
variables, and translate logical reasoning into mathematics.

Suggestions, technical discussions, and constructive improvements are
welcome—especially from students, educators, and researchers interested in
mathematical modeling, scientific computing, and the process of turning an
academic idea into a reproducible project.
